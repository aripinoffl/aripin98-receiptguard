ReceiptGuard 🧾

A proof-checked payment release agent — a Wildcard entry for the zkVerify Agent Games, by @aripin98.

The problem

Small teams, DAOs, grant programs and marketplaces release payments and reimbursements on trust: someone checks a claim by eye and clicks pay. That is slow, inconsistent, and impossible to audit afterwards — when a payment is disputed, there is no evidence the release rules were actually followed. ReceiptGuard replaces the eyeball with a deterministic agent whose every decision carries a zero-knowledge proof that the rules were applied exactly as written.

Try it live

Submit a claim. The agent approves or rejects it with a reason code, generates a real Groth16 proof of the decision with the official gatekeeper circuit, and verifies that proof before answering. Try changing the amount above the cap, the balance below the minimum, or the expiry — the proof still verifies, because it proves the rejection too.

How it works

The release policy is deterministic and fail-closed: balance must meet the minimum, the claim must not be expired, the recipient must match the approved receipt, and the amount must be within the cap — checked in exactly that order, with the same boundary conditions the circuit enforces. The decision bit and the claim fields become the public signals of a Groth16 proof; anyone can verify, with the circuit's verification key, that the stated decision is the only one the rules allow for those fields — without seeing the private salt.
